Asset duration of the pool against the net duration implied by disclosed sensitivities (years)

The asset duration is the estimated duration of the CSM and surplus portfolios together. The implied net duration divides each insurer's disclosed after-tax change in equity for a rate move by the pool and grosses it up for tax, so it includes liabilities measured at current rates, the CSM's own sensitivity and hedges. A net duration near zero means the IFRS balance sheet is close to immune to parallel rate moves. RBC Life publishes no rate sensitivity.

How the estimates are built

  1. Class durations. Each debt maturity bucket in the notes gets a representative term (table below) and the modified duration of a par bond at a flat —% yield. Equity, real estate and alternatives carry no rate duration. With no maturity table, bonds take a proxy from the insurer's liability mix in its OSFI returns.
  2. CSM run-off duration. The disclosed CSM recognition schedule is spread evenly within each bucket and the open-ended last bucket decays geometrically; the Macaulay duration of that stream is the run-off duration. Without a schedule, a geometric run-off at the 2025 release rate is used, scaled by —, the median ratio of the two methods where both can be computed.
  3. Pool. CSM portfolio plus surplus portfolio equals net CSM plus equity less goodwill and intangibles. Where an insurer separates these assets (FVOCI and amortized cost designation, or Beneva's assets outside its matching portfolio) the pool takes that mix and any shortfall is filled at general-account weights; otherwise the pool mirrors the general account. Designated fixed income with no maturity data takes the peer median of — years.
  4. Split. Surplus takes the pool's equity, real estate and alternatives first; the CSM portfolio takes fixed income; the remaining fixed income is shared in proportion.
  5. Check. Where the after-tax rate sensitivity of equity is disclosed, it is grossed up at a —% tax rate and divided by the pool to give its implied net duration.

RBC Life publishes no maturity table, CSM schedule or rate sensitivity, so its CSM run-off uses its release rate from OSFI returns, its pool is its FVOCI and amortized cost book, and that book's fixed income takes the peer FVOCI duration. Its unrealized gain on FVOCI debt, when most peers show losses, points to a short or recently bought book, which fits that assumption.
