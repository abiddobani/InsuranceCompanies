What the numbers say about RBC Life

  • RBC Life is the one large insurer whose accounting designation lines up with its CSM and surplus: C$—MM at FVOCI and amortized cost against C$—MM of equity and C$—MM of Tier 1 CSM. Its portfolios can be read from the regulatory return; most peers need the waterfall.
  • The book is liquid and plain: about two thirds of its fixed income is federal and provincial bonds, and —% of the surplus portfolio is fixed income. The worst quarter of OCI on FVOCI bonds since November 2024 cost —% of equity.
  • The assets behind the CSM look shorter than the CSM itself: — years against a run-off of — years (the run-off schedule is published for RBC's insurance group, not the entity). When yields fall, income on these assets resets lower before the CSM is released.
  • The duration evidence is thin. RBC Life publishes no maturity table, OCI sensitivity or limit for the entity, where BMO Life, Sun Life, Manulife and Beneva publish an OCI sensitivity. The estimate rests on how OCI moved with yields and spans — to — years.
  • What can be done, as a directional read: lengthening the fixed income behind the CSM toward its run-off would hold yield for longer, at the cost of more OCI movement. At the base duration a 50 basis point rise takes about C$—MM before tax through OCI; each added year of duration adds about C$—MM. Holding the longer assets at amortized cost, as Blumont and BMO Life do for loans and mortgages, would lengthen without adding OCI movement. Sizing either choice needs the key-rate profile and any hedges, which are not public.

How the estimates are built

  1. Blocks and sizes. From the cdn-life-insurer-alm study: Canada only, General Account excluding par, segregated funds and universal life policyholder funds, net of reinsurance. The CSM in the General Account excludes segregated fund and par CSM. Surplus is equity less the study's "other" equity line. iA and Beneva report total CSM only and their pools include par, so they are memo rows; Desjardins has no public balance sheet.
  2. Assigning assets. The study's approved rule: A where a filer discloses the split, otherwise B, with C as a sensitivity. A is used for Foresters (its table of assets supporting surplus) and for RBC Life and Blumont (FVOCI and amortized cost book near 1.0 times equity plus CSM). B gives liabilities the fixed income, backs the CSM pro rata to that mix and leaves Surplus the equity, real estate and other assets first. C pools the CSM with Surplus.
  3. Durations. In order of preference: a disclosed sensitivity of the assets (Empire, BMO Life, Wawanesa, Foresters, and the OCI sensitivity of FVOCI bonds at BMO Life and Beneva); maturity tables, at the modified duration of a par bond at — with the open band set between 5 and 15 years past its start; the realised sensitivity of OCI to the 5-year Canada yield (RBC Life, Co-operators Life); and peer medians where nothing is published (Blumont, and RBC Life and Co-operators Life under the waterfall).
  4. Surplus fixed income. Where an insurer shows a surplus-type book, that duration is used. Otherwise the whole-book duration is the base, an upper bound, and the median of the disclosed surplus books (—) is the low case.
  5. CSM run-off. The study's rule: weighted average years to recognition, band midpoints, open band at its start plus five years, undiscounted. Where no schedule exists (Co-operators Life, Wawanesa, Blumont) it is estimated from the 2025 release rate.
  6. Not done here. Key-rate durations, hedges and a full asset and liability projection need data that is not public; the study lists them as next steps. Rate sensitivities are quoted as disclosed, one risk at a time.
