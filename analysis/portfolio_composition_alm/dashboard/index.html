What changed from the October 28 version

  • Basis: the ALM study compares the General Account (GenAcct), which is Canadian business, net of reinsurance, after taking out participating accounts, universal life (UL) policyholder funds and segregated funds. The October 28 version compared whole general funds and took par out for two insurers only, so equity and real estate held for policyholders counted as the insurer's own risk.
  • Dates: fiscal 2025 year-ends, which are October 31, 2025 for RBC Life and BMO Life and December 31, 2025 for the others.
  • Big 3: Manulife and Sun Life Canada are assumed to follow the global mix (est); Canada Life uses its disclosed Canada column.
  • Taxonomy: the study's Level 1, 2 and 3 asset classes. Private placements stay in illiquid term loans.
  • New: the liabilities each General Account funds, and how the asset mix lines up against them (sections 2 and 5).
  • Coverage: 19 insurers have a General Account mix. Desjardins Insurance publishes none for its life insurer, and Blumont Annuity is added from the study's peer set.

1. Basis: which assets are being compared

The General Account is the part of the balance sheet where the insurer, not the policyholder, carries the investment risk. For some insurers it is nearly all of the invested assets; for Canada Life and Equitable it is under a third, because their participating accounts are so large.

Definitions used on this page
  • General Account (GenAcct): invested assets for Canadian business, net of reinsurance, excluding participating accounts, UL policyholder funds and segregated funds.
  • Level 1: internally managed, externally managed (real estate, infrastructure, private equity and private debt funds) and off-balance-sheet. Level 2 and 3 are the asset classes within them.
  • Illiquid assets: commercial mortgages, private and consumer loans, real estate, infrastructure, private equity, private debt and other invested assets.
  • Outside fixed income: common and preferred shares, pooled fund units, real estate, infrastructure equity, private equity and other invested assets.
  • Long liabilities: individual insurance and group annuities as a share of non-par insurance contract liabilities.
  • Surplus: equity attributed to the non-par General Account under the study's default waterfall, in which fixed income and mortgages back liabilities first and the CSM is backed pro rata.
  • Peer median: the median of the other insurers that publish the measure; RBC Life is not in it.
