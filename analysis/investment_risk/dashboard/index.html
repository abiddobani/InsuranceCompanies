LICAT market risk capital

Market risk requirement for non-participating products as a share of invested assets, OSFI LCQ fiscal 2025 Q4. It captures interest rate, equity, real estate and currency risk under the regulator’s standard shocks, so it is the one market risk measure every OSFI filer reports on the same basis.

Public equity, real estate and private equity

Share of the latest general-account portfolio in common and preferred shares, real estate, infrastructure equity and other private equity (portfolio composition work). These are the assets behind the equity and alternatives shocks.

Doff’s time-bucket view of liquidity applied to the asset side: how much of each latest general-account portfolio could be sold or repo’d within days, within weeks, or only over months. Policy loans sit with private loans.

Liquidity-weighted assets

Doff’s weighted liquidity ratio uses assets weighted by how readily they turn into cash. Weights here are illustrative: cash 100%, government bonds 95%, public corporate bonds 85%, ABS and listed shares 70%, mortgages and private liquid loans 50%, private placements and other private debt 40%, real estate and private equity 20%, policy loans 0%. Liability-side outflows are not public, so this is the numerator only, as a share of the portfolio.

Repo funding

Securities sold under repurchase agreements as a share of the general-account portfolio, OSFI LF3 fiscal 2025 (iA and Manulife from their Q2 2026 notes). Repos raise contingent liquidity needs: collateral calls rise when rates move.

Derivative notionals

Gross notional of interest rate, currency, equity and credit derivatives as a share of the portfolio, OSFI LF3 fiscal 2025 (iA from its Q2 2026 notes). A rough gauge of margin and collateral calls under stress.

Published liquid-asset measures

Only two peers publish a liquid-asset total. Manulife counts cash plus investment-grade government, corporate and securitized bonds and public equities; Great-West Lifeco counts net liquid assets after removing pledged and non-liquid holdings. Both are shown against invested assets. Sun Life and iA describe liquidity stress tests without publishing the ratios.

The large listed groups disclose a full set of market sensitivities and rating tables; most smaller peers publish only partial notes; RBC Life, BMO Life, ivari, Co-operators Life, Canadian Premier and Primerica publish no investment risk metrics of their own, so OSFI returns are the only view. Canada Protection Plan reports inside Foresters. Desjardins, Humania and UV publish little at the life-company level.

How the metrics map to the framework

  • Market risk. Doff measures market risk as the change in the market-value balance sheet under shocks to rates, spreads, equity and real estate. The disclosed IFRS 17 sensitivities are that measure for earnings and the capital ratio; LICAT market risk capital is the regulator’s standard version of the same shocks.
  • Credit risk. Doff builds credit risk from ratings (probability of default), loss given default and concentration. Here: rating mix, below-investment-grade share, private credit and mortgage share (higher loss given default uncertainty), LICAT credit risk capital, credit loss allowances, problem mortgages and sector concentration.
  • Liquidity risk. Doff’s liquidity ratio is liquid assets over liquid liabilities by time bucket. Insurers do not publish the liability side, so the dashboard shows the asset side: liquidity tiers, a liquidity-weighted asset share, repo funding and derivative notionals that drive collateral calls.
  • Scope. OSFI figures are for the regulated legal entity (Canada Life Assurance, Manufacturers Life, Sun Life Assurance of Canada), while their disclosed sensitivities are group-wide. LICAT credit and market capital cover non-participating products only.
