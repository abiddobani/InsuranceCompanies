How the measures are built

  • Framework. The cdn-life-insurer-alm repo is the method throughout. It looks at Canadian business net of reinsurance, and at the General Account after removing participating accounts, segregated funds and universal life policyholder funds, because investment results on those pass through to policyholders.
  • Capital. LICAT required capital by category comes from the OSFI LCQ return. Credit, market and insurance requirements cover non-participating business. The floor on the capital charts is the level the ALM study assumes, not a regulatory or company target.
  • Two peer sets. General Account measures use the median of the eleven other OSFI peers. LICAT measures use the median of the eight other all-Canada filers, because the Manulife, Sun Life and Canada Life entities include foreign business.
  • Market risk. Asset mix follows the repo’s block allocation at its third level. Sensitivities are each insurer’s own published figures for its own shock; they are never added across risks and never rescaled.
  • Credit risk. Private credit, below-investment-grade bonds, sector mix and credit loss allowances come from OSFI LF2 and LF3 for the legal entity.
  • Liquidity and funding. Liquid assets are cash, public bonds and public shares, net of bonds delivered as repo collateral. Repos are treated as a rollover and collateral risk, in line with LICAT sections 2.1.2.6 and 3.5, not as a capital deduction.
  • Estimates. Items marked est are the repo’s estimates: the Manulife and Sun Life Canada asset mix, Equitable’s universal life removal, and the RBC Life sensitivity proxy.
  • Fallback. The repo holds no sensitivities for iA, Beneva or Desjardins, so those rows are carried from the earlier benchmark as published.
